# Origami Competitive Landscape and Research Report
Date: 2026-07-28. Scope: products, failures, papers, gap analysis for a local-first personal AI knowledge base on macOS.

## Executive Summary

- Chat-with-your-documents is fully commoditized. AnythingLLM, Open WebUI, Msty, LM Studio, Jan, and free entrants like Hyperlink by Nexa AI all ship local RAG over personal files. Origami cannot win on "ask questions about your PDFs."
- The always-on memory category burned down in front of everyone. Rewind died inside Meta (Dec 2025), Microsoft Recall remains distrusted after two years of patches, and screenpipe pivoted to agent infrastructure. Deliberate, user-initiated capture is the surviving posture, and nobody owns it.
- Personal agents went viral and then went septic. OpenClaw hit 180K stars, then a one-click RCE (CVE-2026-25253) and the ClawHavoc supply-chain campaign made "personal AI agent" synonymous with breach. Security-by-construction is now a legible consumer story, and no consumer product ships it as a visible feature.
- The research literature validates Origami's two structural bets: taint-based egress control matches Meta's Agents Rule of Two and DeepMind's CaMeL direction (still unshipped in any harness as of mid-2026), and per-segment provenance plus bi-temporal timestamps matches Zep/Graphiti. Most memory-system papers (Mem0, A-MEM, HippoRAG) should not change the roadmap.
- The defensible gap is an intersection, not a single feature: provenance-first multimodal corpus + visible agent security + no-always-on screenshot intelligence + Obsidian-grade portability, on one local store. Each piece alone is copyable or ignorable; together they are a position no funded competitor is structurally able to take, because their businesses require cloud, capture, or credits.

## Part 2: Failure Patterns

1. **Always-on capture sells anxiety bundled with the product.** Recall needed opt-in, Windows Hello, BitLocker, and a sensitive-data filter, and independent tests in Aug 2025 still found the filter leaking credit cards and SSNs; 2026 research showed malware could exfiltrate the store. Signal shipped a DRM flag specifically to blind it. Rewind's Mac recorder never escaped the "creepy" framing and died in an acquihire. Lesson: background recording makes the user defend the product to themselves daily.
2. **Cloud processing of intimate data caps trust and geography.** Limitless processed pendant audio in the cloud; on acquisition it exited the EU, UK, and five other regions overnight, and stopped hardware sales. Khoj shut its hosted tier. Personal-corpus products that touch servers inherit compliance surface they cannot afford.
3. **Company death means data death, and buyers noticed.** Omnivore gave users two weeks before deleting everything. Pocket took 35M users down with it. Skiff shut inside a year of acquisition. PKM buyer behavior measurably shifted to "which tool still exists in 2030," favoring open source and profitable indies. This is a tailwind for an MIT-licensed local app with plain files.
4. **Metering AI on the user's own data breeds resentment.** Notion's $10 per 1K agent credits on top of $20/mo, Tana's credit tiers, and Heptabase's $72/mo top tier all generate pricing backlash threads. Msty had to make its core free. Open WebUI's license grab triggered fork revolts. Users tolerate paying for models; they resent paying rent on their own corpus.
5. **Security bolted on after virality fails structurally.** OpenClaw launched with auth disabled on port 8080, docs admitting no secure setup exists, then took a critical RCE and a supply-chain malware campaign within weeks of going viral. Cisco, IBM X-Force, and half the security industry now use it as the case study. Anthropic's own browser-agent testing showed a 31.5% hijack rate before safeguards.
6. **Solo OSS without a distinct wedge dies quietly.** Reor was exactly "local AI notes with RAG," competently built, 2024 Show HN darling, archived March 2026. Being local and open is not a wedge when twenty products are local and open.
7. **Pivot gravity pulls personal tools toward enterprise meetings and agent infra**, because that is where revenue is (Granola to enterprise, screenpipe to work SOPs, Mem to thought partner). The personal corpus itself keeps getting abandoned mid-market.

## Part 3: Research Papers

Agentic memory:
- **MemGPT (arXiv 2310.08560, now Letta).** OS-style paged memory; the agent manages its own context hierarchy. Verdict: no roadmap change. Origami's corpus is the memory; conversation-state paging is a different problem.
- **Mem0 (arXiv 2504.19413, ECAI 2025).** Extract-and-consolidate salient facts; big latency wins over full-context on LoCoMo. Verdict: no change now. Also note LoCoMo results are contested (Zep and Mem0 publicly disputed each other's evals), so treat all memory-benchmark marketing skeptically.
- **Zep / Graphiti (arXiv 2501.13956).** Bi-temporal knowledge graph, validity intervals on every edge; +18.5% on LongMemEval tasks. Verdict: partial adoption, already done. The event-time vs ingestion-time split directly validates Origami's `created_at`/`ingested_at`. Do not adopt the KG engine itself: LLM-based entity extraction at ingest is exactly the per-item generative cost COST_MODEL exists to kill.
- **HippoRAG 1/2 (NeurIPS 2024; arXiv 2502.14802).** KG + Personalized PageRank for multi-hop and associative recall; HippoRAG 2 frames it as continual memory. Verdict: watchlist. A future "related items" view could use it, but it needs OpenIE at ingest. Not now.
- **A-MEM (arXiv 2502.12110).** Zettelkasten-style LLM-maintained note links. Verdict: no change. Academically interesting, economically wrong for local-first.
- **LongMemEval (ICLR 2025; arXiv 2410.10813) and LongMemEval-V2 (2026).** Commercial assistants drop 30% accuracy on sustained-interaction memory; taxonomy: extraction, multi-session reasoning, temporal reasoning, updates, abstention. Verdict: yes, act. Build a 50-question Origami eval in this style over a synthetic personal corpus. Temporal reasoning and abstention are the two failure modes personal KBs actually hit. Cheap, high leverage.

Multimodal retrieval:
- **ColPali (arXiv 2407.01449, ICLR 2025) and ColQwen2/2.5.** Late-interaction retrieval over page images beats OCR+embed pipelines on visually rich documents (ViDoRe); by 2026 it is the default recommendation for production visual-document RAG. Verdict: changes the roadmap, carefully. Origami's VLM-describe-then-embed discards layout and inherits caption confabulation. Full ColPali is heavy for consumer Macs (multi-vector storage blowup, 2B+ VLM at ingest), but the Segment schema's per-segment `embedding_model` and `modality` fields mean a `page_image` multi-vector modality can be added later without re-architecture. Design for it now, ship it when NanoVDR-style distilled retrievers (arXiv 2603.12824) make it laptop-cheap.
- Interim implication: keep OCR text as a first-class segment alongside the VLM caption, never caption-only. OCR is `extracted`, caption is `generated`; the distinction is exactly Origami's `content_source` field.

Security:
- **Lethal trifecta (Willison, Jun 2025).** Private data + untrusted content + exfiltration channel; remove one leg per execution path. Verdict: already the load-bearing design. Keep.
- **CaMeL (DeepMind, arXiv 2503.18813).** Capabilities and information-flow control around a dual-LLM interpreter; 77% of AgentDojo solved with provable security. As of mid-2026, no shipped harness implements it. Verdict: validates direction, do not implement fully. Origami's coarse "tainted turn loses egress" is the shippable 80%.
- **Design Patterns for Securing LLM Agents (arXiv 2506.08837).** Six patterns; context-minimization and plan-then-execute are the two worth borrowing for the connector era. Verdict: selective adoption.
- **Agents Rule of Two (Meta, late 2025).** At most two of the three trifecta legs per session. Verdict: external validation that Origami's mechanism is the industry-converged answer; cite it in docs and marketing.
- **The Attacker Moves Second (2025).** Adaptive attacks defeated published detection-style defenses. Verdict: confirms that classifier or prompt-based injection defense is a dead end; structural egress control is the right bet.
- **Platform guidance (2026).** OWASP Agentic Top 10 (Dec 2025) puts goal hijacking at #1; Anthropic and OpenAI both shipped sandboxed managed-agent runtimes with credential scoping in April-May 2026. Verdict: permissioning is becoming table stakes at the platform layer, but nobody has made it a visible consumer feature in a PKM.

Personal-data RAG evaluation: thin literature. LongMemEval and AgentDojo are the usable instruments; there is no accepted benchmark for retrieval over a heterogeneous personal corpus (screenshots + calendar + messages). That absence is itself an opportunity: publishing a small open benchmark would buy credibility cheaply.

## Part 4: Gap Analysis

**Commodity (do not compete on):** chat-with-docs, basic hybrid RAG, model management UX, meeting transcription (Granola cloud, Hyprnote local), self-hosted chat UIs. All free or near-free, several with VC subsidy.

**Contested (enter only with an angle):** always-on screen memory (screenpipe with YC money, Recall with OS distribution, Meta absorbing Rewind's team); general personal agent runtimes (OpenClaw, Hermes at 180K+ stars); cloud AI workspaces (Notion, Mem, Granola with $192M raised). A solo project loses all three head-on.

**Underserved, ranked. Each with supporting evidence and the strongest attack against it.**

**1. Agent security as a visible, user-facing feature: taint badges on retrieved content, and a UI state that says "this answer touched untrusted content, so network and send are off for this turn."**
Evidence: OpenClaw's CVE and ClawHavoc made agent insecurity front-page legible; OWASP ranks goal hijacking #1; Meta's Rule of Two and CaMeL converge on exactly Origami's mechanism, and NeuralTrust confirms no harness has shipped CaMeL ten months on. The mechanism is designed (ARCHITECTURE_V2 §3) before any connector exists, which no retrofit competitor can claim.
Attack: users do not buy security, they buy outcomes; OpenClaw grew to 180K stars while being a security dumpster fire, which is direct evidence the target market does not select on this. The taint restriction also degrades the product (the README itself tags the ergonomics UNVERIFIED): "research this thing from my messages" is a legitimate request the rule blocks, and every confirmation dialog erodes the feature's halo. And Apple could ship OS-level agent permissioning that makes app-level taint look homemade. Mitigation: sell it as "why Origami can safely read your messages when nothing else can," an enabler of connectors, not a padlock icon.

**2. Screenshot intelligence without always-on recording: deliberate capture, drag-in, and Photos-library import, with OCR + caption both stored and both attributed.**
Evidence: three years of documented anxiety around Recall and Rewind, with Recall's filter still leaking in Aug 2025 tests and its store extractable by malware in 2026; screenpipe abandoned the personal niche for work-agent infra; the surviving demand (people screenshot constantly, then lose them) is served by nobody with corpus-grade modeling. Hyprnote proved "the private version of X" is a fundable, adoptable wedge.
Attack: Apple Intelligence already OCRs screenshots system-wide and Spotlight search improves every WWDC, so the platform may absorb the basic value; deliberate capture may be too low-frequency to form a habit, and the anxiety evidence concerns background recording, not proof anyone wants curated capture. Hyperlink already indexes screenshot folders on-device for free. Mitigation: the differentiator is not finding screenshots, it is screenshots becoming first-class cited sources inside a reasoning agent, which Spotlight and Hyperlink do not attempt.

**3. Provenance-first retrieval honesty: every citation says whether it quotes extracted text or a model-generated description, which model embedded it, and where in the original it lives.**
Evidence: the ColPali literature exists precisely because describe-then-embed pipelines lose the document; `content_source` separating extracted from generated is designed in; no consumer product surfaces "this is a caption, not the source" even though confabulated citations are the top complaint against AI answers over personal data.
Attack: this is plumbing users may never consciously notice, and a competitor can bolt a "source: OCR" label on in a sprint; honesty labels do not retain users, answers do. Weakest evidence of the five: no user research shows anyone churns over caption-vs-source confusion. Mitigation: it compounds with #1 and #2 rather than standing alone, and it is nearly free since the schema already carries it.

**4. Longevity positioning: MIT license, no account, plain files and SQLite/Chroma on disk, corpus never leaves the machine.**
Evidence: Omnivore deleted user data on two weeks' notice, Pocket and Skiff died, Khoj's cloud died, Reor died, and PKM buyers now explicitly ask "will this exist in 2030"; the market's stated preference moved to open source and indie-profitable.
Attack: Obsidian already owns this position with a decade of trust, a million users, and a plugin ecosystem, and within the OSS niche "local + MIT + markdown" is table stakes claimed by every new entrant; worse, a solo project is itself the longevity risk (Reor is the proof), so the claim invites the exact scrutiny it markets. Mitigation: make exportability demonstrable (one-command full export) rather than promised, and position against cloud PKMs, not against Obsidian.

**5. Cost transparency: local by default with zero marginal cost, and when a BYO key is attached, a visible per-query and per-ingest cost meter.**
Evidence: Notion's credit metering, Tana's credit tiers, and Heptabase's AI pricing ladder generate steady resentment; Msty had to free its core; Origami's COST_MODEL work means the numbers already exist internally.
Attack: in a local-only default the meter mostly reads $0.00, making the feature decorative; the users who attach keys are power users who already run LiteLLM dashboards; and if local models keep improving, the whole problem evaporates, taking the differentiator with it. Lowest ceiling of the five. Mitigation: fold it into onboarding honesty ("this app costs you electricity") rather than treating it as a headline feature.

**Synthesis.** No single differentiator survives its own attack cleanly. The defensible position is the intersection: the only tool where a multimodal personal corpus is provenance-tagged at ingest, screenshots are deliberate and cited, and the agent's security posture is structural and visible, in an MIT app whose data never leaves the machine. Funded competitors are structurally barred from copying the whole bundle: Notion/Granola/Mem need cloud, screenpipe/Recall need always-on capture, OpenClaw/Hermes need maximal agent permissions, and Obsidian plugins cannot control the ingest path well enough to guarantee taint coverage. The bundle is the moat; ship the LongMemEval-style eval and the taint UI first, because those are the two artifacts nobody else has.
